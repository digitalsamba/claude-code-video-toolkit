AI clips that don't freeze or loop

An image-to-video clip runs about five seconds. A shot often runs seven to eleven. What fills the gap decides whether a generated person looks alive.

We hit this making comedy shorts with animated photos: an LTX clip of a person laughing is ~5 s, and the shot it sits in is longer. Three ways to fill it, measured on the same 11-second shot:

FillFrozen framesFrames that repeat an earlier oneHow it reads
Hold the last frame63%0%the video paused
Play the tail back and forthnot measured29%a loop, a rewind
Continue the clip from its own frame0%0%one continuous take

The raw LTX clips measured 0% repeats, so the loop was ours, not the model's.

The fix: continue, don't stretch

Take the clip's last good frame, run image-to-video again from it with the same prompt, and join the two with a cross-fade of about 0.12 s. Repeat for longer shots. Each extension is one more clip, about $0.08 on Modal. The toolkit does it in one command:

uv run tools/ltx_extend.py --input people/rob.mp4 --at 4.8 \
    --prompt "A fixed view that never moves ... the same man keeps laughing ... no text" \
    --output people/rob-long.mp4          # --times 2 for a third link

"Last good frame" matters: continuing from an earlier frame also cuts out a bad stretch. A feet-up kick that grew a third leg never plays if the continuation starts before it.

What we learned the hard way

  • Never continue from a close-up. With only a face to go on, the model invents a new scene.
  • Lock the framing in words ("a fixed view that never moves, no zoom, no cuts"), but don't say "tripod": it draws one.
  • Seek before the end. Grabbing the very last frame of a clip with ffmpeg returns nothing; take the last frame before the seam.
  • Keep what you paid for. A chain that fails at link two should keep link one, keyed on its prompt, seam and seed, so a re-run doesn't pay twice.
  • A slightly short clip can be slowed, down to about 0.8x before faces look slow-motion; beyond that, extend.
  • Contact-sheet every continuation. A continuation can still cut to a stranger at its end.

Catching it automatically

A whole-frame "is anything moving?" check misses a frozen face, because something else on screen is always moving. The toolkit's qc.py --render now looks per region for motion that stops dead: part of the frame moving for 3 s or more, then frozen for 2 s or more while the rest of the video goes on. On four renders it found every clip freeze in the old cuts and none in the fixed ones.
